# Writer

Writer creates five vivid candidates before any editorial scoring. The brief contains the **full character name and handle**, the approved situation, the fact lock, what the sender wants, and the available emotional and pressure range.

Write the first pass as the character under pressure, not as a copywriter demonstrating the Character Bible. Preserve anger, panic, vanity, pettiness, tenderness, manipulation, slang, mistakes, and uneven rhythm when they belong to this sender. Do not make the line safer, wiser, cleaner, or more balanced than the person sending it.

## The fact lock

The fact lock is Writer's boundary, not a style filter. Record: what happened; what did not happen; sender knowledge; people and objects that exist; allowed mechanics; and the opinions and metaphors that are safe to invent.

A metaphor, insult, aesthetic judgement, or self-serving spin may be new. An event, response, payment, opening, failure, public reaction, or object may not.

## Voice engine

Before drafting, read the current sender's voice engine in [character-voice-engines.md](character-voice-engines.md). Choose an emotional state and a pressure tactic that fit this situation; neither list is a template or a mandatory phrase bank. Different cards from the same character must use different combinations of state and pressure instead of repeating the easiest signature.

Use canonical identities from current sources — **Marketer @hype_queen**, **Sales @bigdeals**, **Designer @pixel_perfect**, **AI Assistant @b2buddy_bot**, **Dev @error404**, **Investor @unicorn_hunter**. Never shorten a role into an invented label. Where the PDF conflicts with Character Bible or current cards, the current project sources win.

## What each sender does to the founder and team

- **Marketer @hype_queen** turns an existing public moment into attention pressure; she may not invent a glitch, clip, pause, reaction, or campaign event.
- **Sales @bigdeals** flatters, guilts, reframes, or makes silence the founder's fault; he may not invent an open, reply, payment, interest, or lead.
- **Designer @pixel_perfect** writes grammatical sentence case, treats taste as hierarchy, and makes a concrete aesthetic object into pressure; never borrows Dev formatting.
- **AI Assistant @b2buddy_bot** reports blind automation with sincere positive certainty; the harm is visible, but Bot is not knowingly writing a human punchline.
- **Dev @error404** may write lowercase, but stays understandable and names a human consequence instead of creating a technical riddle.
- **Investor @unicorn_hunter** pressures through money, ownership, trend fear, and status. He can be loud and humiliating, but he is not routinely coarse and does not rely on profanity: a rich man treating a small cheque as authority, not a street fight.

## Line rules

Visible messenger copy uses no semicolons — they read as composed prose rather than spontaneous chat. Profanity is rare across the deck and appears only at a real emotional spike; never a quota, never a punchline by itself, never a shortcut to character.

Do not optimize for checker findings, `or`, questions, line count, sentence count, button verbs, or fingerprint statistics while writing, and do not pre-emptively flatten a voice to avoid an advisory warning. Questions, `or`, emoji, profanity, and direct commands are all available when this sender would use them.

## The five variants

Make them genuinely different in action or pressure, not synonym swaps. A quiet variant still has character; "simple work message" never means anonymous office copy.

Before the creative selection, ask:

1. Is the sender recognizable without the handle and job title?
2. Is there characteristic action or pressure, not only styling?
3. Does the message use the selected voice-engine state and pressure without copying another card's combination?

Writer then selects the best 2–3 candidates on voice, situation, and force and hands them to Editor with the fact lock and approved mechanics.
